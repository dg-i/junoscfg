Anonymization keeps a wildcard name in a configuration group a pattern. `groups GR-BGP protocols bgp group <*>` used to become `group group_<hash>` under `--anonymize-groups`, a BGP group of its own that applied the group to no BGP group; login users, SNMP communities and SSH keys named `<*>` were renamed the same way. `<*>` now passes every rule. Any other wildcard keeps its angle brackets and its text is replaced as the names at its place are: `<EBGP>` names the renamed group `EBGP`, `<203.0.114.7>` the neighbour's replacement (it used to keep the address), and `<203.0.114.*>` becomes the pattern of that prefix's replacement, as `<peer-203.0.114.*>` does under `--anonymize-ips-in-strings`. A wildcard that, anonymized, selects other names than before among those the configuration holds at its place (`<EBGP-*>` beside `EBGP-A` under `--anonymize-groups`), or whose text no rule could rewrite (an address pattern that is no pattern of whole octets, or whose prefix a preserved or reserved block cuts; a sensitive word behind a class such as `<[Aa]cme-*>`; an AS number behind `?` under `--anonymize-as-numbers-in-strings`), is named by schema path in the new `AnonymizeResult.patterns` and in an `anonymize:` warning that quotes no value. Revert restores every wildcard in its own spelling.
